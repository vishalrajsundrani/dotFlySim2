# Cameras and the wrapper surface: aircraft vs simulation

Written for the visual-localisation work. The question was what actually
differs between the simulation and the recording from the real aircraft, and
what that means for modelling the payload and fisheye cameras.

Full tables are in `dotFlySim2_surface_comparison.xlsx` (this directory), generated by
`tools/surface_compare.py` so they can be regenerated rather than trusted.

---

## The short answer

**The bag cannot tell you how to model the fisheyes, because it does not
contain them.** `perception_stereo_left_stream` and
`perception_stereo_right_stream` are not in the recording at all — not empty,
absent. The perception node was started as a lifecycle node
(`/wrapper/perception_node/transition_event` exists) but `start_perception`
was never called, so that flight carries no stereo imagery whatsoever.

What the bag *does* give you is the payload stream, and it holds one surprise
worth designing around.

For the fisheyes the reference has to be DJI's published sensing
specification, and measured against it the simulation's perception cameras are
wrong in a way that matters specifically for localisation: **they are pinhole
cameras, not fisheyes.**

## 1. Three sources, which do not mean the same thing

| source | what it is | what a blank means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **documented** | `tools/psdk_surface.py`, transcribed from the PSDK topics/services PDF | the wrapper does not offer it |
| **recorded** | `bag_000026`, one 184 s flight | *only* that it was off, or not populated in that mode — not that the aircraft cannot |
| **simulated** | read from `bridge/simty/registry.py` | the simulation genuinely does not produce it |

Totals: 58 topics in the bag, 34 wrapper topics from the simulation, 54
documented; all 56 documented services are defined, 42 advertised by
default (see §3).

## 2. Topic differences

**13 topics the aircraft published with real data that the simulation does not
produce at all.** All are in the bridge's own `SYNTHESIS_GAP`, so they are
known gaps rather than surprises:

| topic | rate in the bag | relevance to localisation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `acceleration_body_raw`, `acceleration_body_fused`, `acceleration_ground_fused` | 43.3 Hz | accel is available via `imu`; see §6 |
| `angular_rate_ground_fused` | 43.4 Hz | ENU-rotated rates |
| `magnetic_field` | 43.4 Hz | **yes** — heading prior for a VIO/GNSS fusion |
| `rtk_position_info`, `rtk_velocity`, `rtk_yaw`, `rtk_yaw_info`, `rtk_connection_status` | 4.5 Hz | **yes** — RTK yaw is a strong absolute heading reference |
| `esc_data`, `motor_start_error`, `gps_signal_level` | 13–18 Hz | no |

**6 topics the aircraft advertises and never populates** (0 messages in 184 s):
`battery`, `gimbal_status`, `gps_control_level`, `home_point_altitude`,
`landing_gear_status`, and — importantly — `perception_camera_parameters`.

**Present and matching**, and useful to you: `visual_odometry`
(`nav_msgs/Odometry`, 43.4 Hz) and `gimbal_angles` (43.6 Hz). DJI's own VO
solution is in the bag, which gives you a baseline to compare your system
against.

## 3. Service differences

> **Corrected 2026-10-07.** An earlier revision of this section said 24 of the
> 56 documented services were "not served". That was wrong, and the fault was
> in `tools/surface_compare.py`: it matched only literal
> `ServiceRoute("key", "name", ...)` calls and so missed every route built in
> a loop — the ten obstacle-avoidance pairs (an f-string over five directions)
> and the fourteen camera-settings rows (a comprehension over a table). The
> tool now reads all three declaration forms. What follows is the measured
> position.

**All 56 documented services are defined.** None is missing.

**42 are advertised by default.** The remaining 14 ship `enabled=False`, and a
disabled route is never created (`node._realise_service` returns early), so a
caller gets `service not available` exactly as if it did not exist:

| shipped disabled | why |
| --- | --- |
| `camera_{set,get}_aperture`, `_iso`, `_shutter_speed`, `_exposure_mode_ev`, `_focus_mode`, `_focus_target`, `_focus_ring_value` | they store a value and change no pixel; the simulated optics are fixed |

That is a deliberate choice rather than a gap: enabling them would let a
mission believe it had changed an exposure that nothing acted on. Turn them on
from walker's SERVICES screen if a payload needs them to answer at start-up.

**Two services exist here that psdk_ros2's PDF list does not name**:
`gimbal_reset` and `gimbal_set_mode`. Both are real wrapper services, present
in the online documentation; the PDF transcription in `tools/psdk_surface.py`
is simply incomplete. `tools/surface_compare.py --check` allows exactly these
two and fails on anything else.

Neither group blocks visual localisation. Note that a rosbag records topics,
not service calls, so the aircraft column cannot exist here — this is
documentation against simulation only.

## 4. The payload camera, measured from the bag

| property | value |
| --- | --- |
| topic | `/wrapper/psdk_ros2/main_camera_stream`, `sensor_msgs/Image` |
| encoding | `rgb8`, `is_bigendian=0`, `step = 3·width` |
| frames | 3,898 over 183.9 s |
| rate | **23.5 Hz mean, 28.6 Hz median**, p5–p95 interval 21–71 ms |
| gaps | 9 gaps > 0.5 s, 23.7 s total (the same recorder stalls the IMU work found) |

**The resolution changes during the flight.** 2,243 frames at 1920×1080 and
1,655 at 1440×1080 — 16:9 and 4:3 — switching five times:

```
t =   0.00 s   1440x1080
t =   2.72 s   1920x1080
t =   3.76 s   1440x1080
t =  29.51 s   1920x1080
t = 139.03 s   1440x1080
```

The simulation emits one fixed size forever (`m4e_wide_videofhd`, 1920×1080 at
30 Hz). **A consumer that reads intrinsics from the first frame and caches
them works in simulation and is wrong on the aircraft** the first time the
operator changes camera mode. If your localisation front end touches the
payload camera at all, handle a resolution change at runtime, and expect
~23 Hz rather than 30.

The simulation's payload model is otherwise rich — 12 sensors: three lenses
(wide 71.5°, medium-tele 28.3°, tele 12.0° horizontal) × four tiers (photo,
4K, FHD, preview).

## 5. The fisheyes — where the real problems are

Nothing in the bag. Against DJI's specification:

| direction | real FOV (H×V) | modelled in SDF | sim resolution | sim FOV | sim projection |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FRONT | 90° × **135°** | `perception_front_left/right` | 704×704 | 90° × 90° | **pinhole** |
| REAR | 90° × **135°** | `perception_back_left/right` | 704×704 | 90° × 90° | **pinhole** |
| LEFT / RIGHT | 90° × 90° | `perception_left`, `perception_right` | 704×704 | 90° × 90° | **pinhole** |
| DOWN | **160° × 160°** | `perception_down` | 640×640 | 160° | **pinhole** |
| UP | no stereo pair | not modelled | — | — | — |

Four problems, in the order they will hurt you:

**1. They are pinhole cameras, not fisheyes.** Every perception sensor uses
Gazebo's default rectilinear projection. For the 90° pairs that is merely
wrong at the edges; for the 160° downward camera it is not representable at
all — a rectilinear projection diverges towards 180°, so edge pixels are
stretched without bound. Gazebo has `wideanglecamera` with
`<lens><type>equidistant</type>` (or `equisolid_angle`) for exactly this.
Until it changes, **any undistortion, feature detector tuning, or depth
estimate calibrated in simulation is calibrated against a lens the aircraft
does not have.**

**2. The forward and backward pairs have the wrong vertical field of view.**
The sensors are square with a single 90° horizontal FOV, so their vertical FOV
is also 90°. DJI specifies **135°** vertically for front and rear. That is a
third less vertical coverage than the real aircraft, which directly reduces
how much structure a VIO front end sees during climb and descent. The lateral
pairs at 90×90 are already correct.

**3. Only FRONT is wired.** Five of the seven sensors exist in the SDF and
reach nothing: `walker.toml` lists only `perception_front_left/right`, and
`registry._PERCEPTION_SIMULATED` is `("FRONT",)`. `start_perception` refuses
the other directions explicitly rather than succeeding and publishing silence
— which is the right behaviour — but it means **omnidirectional localisation
cannot be developed against this simulation today.** DOWN is the one to add
first: widest FOV, and the most texture during an inspection hover.

**4. There are no intrinsics on either side.**
`perception_camera_parameters` is advertised by the aircraft and never
populated — the bridge records that the underlying PSDK call segfaults on a
real M4E — so on hardware your calibration has to come from a file. The
simulation should publish the same calibration through the same path, or the
two environments are calibrated differently by construction.

One thing the simulation gets right: the wrapper exposes **one stereo pair at
a time** on two fixed topic names, switched by `start_perception`, and the
simulation mirrors that rather than publishing all pairs at once. Design your
system for one pair at a time.

## 6. Carried over from the IMU work

The accelerometer gravity difference is unresolved and matters more for a
visual-**inertial** system than for anything else:

| | mean of `imu.linear_acceleration` |
| --- | --- |
| aircraft | 0.0036 m/s² — gravity removed by the flight controller |
| simulation | 9.7978 m/s² — gravity present |

A VIO system integrates this term directly. Until it is settled, the two
environments cannot agree. See `IMU_CALIBRATION.md` §7a (this directory) for why it was flagged
rather than decided.

---

## 7. What I recommend, in order

1. **Fisheye projection** on all seven perception sensors (`wideanglecamera`,
   equidistant). Blocks geometric transfer.
2. **Gravity decision** in `sensor_combined_to_imu`. Blocks VIO.
3. **Render DOWN**, then REAR and the laterals.
4. **Publish `camera_info`** from a calibration file, same path both sides.
5. **135° vertical FOV** for FRONT and REAR.
6. **Handle main-camera resolution changes**, or at least document the
   assumption.

Items 1, 3 and 5 are all edits to `models/m4e/model.sdf` plus wiring in
`walker.toml`, `config/bridge.yaml` and the registry. Item 2 is a one-line
decision with wide consequences.

Nothing here has been changed. This is the survey you asked for.
